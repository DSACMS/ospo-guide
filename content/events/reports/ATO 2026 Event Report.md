 ## Overview
Attended the largest open source gathering on the US East Coast, with almost 5,900 registered attendees and over 150 sessions and speakers featured in the program.

Via [https\://2026.allthingsopen.org/about-the-conference](https://2026.allthingsopen.org/about-the-conference):  

“All Things Open is a technology conference focusing on the tools, processes and people making open source possible. Our sophisticated and diverse audience is a technical one that includes designers, developers, decision makers, entrepreneurs and technologists of all types and skill levels.

The conference takes place in Raleigh’s Research Triangle Park (RTP), the world-wide headquarters of leading tech and open source companies.

## Findings/Learnings


**Day 1: Targeted Summits** 

Accessibility Summit 

* [https\://github.com/mgifford/ACCESSIBILITY.md/blob/main/ACCESSIBILITY.md](https://github.com/mgifford/ACCESSIBILITY.md/blob/main/ACCESSIBILITY.md)   
* Accessibility remains a key focus of the open source community, with champions like Mike Gifford and Open Source Accessibility sharing a new accessibility markdown file and checklist for repositories, making note of testing procedures, dependencies, user experiences, contributor requirements, visual assets, release requirements, and more.   
  * Recommendation for the OSPG team \- how might we incorporate an accessibility md file of our own?

**Day 2: AllThingsOpen Main Conference**

- [https\://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/10/introducing-elephant-goldfish-model-plugin.html](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/10/introducing-elephant-goldfish-model-plugin.html)   
* A major focus on the ways AI is transforming open source work, from making code easier to write, the importance of review processes and training LLMs to review PRs, and how teams manage transformed workloads. 

**Day 3: OSI AI Salon**

* [https\://opensource.org/ai-salon](https://opensource.org/ai-salon)  
* Discussions explored the impact of the Open Source AI Definition and its practical application across sectors, while creating space for participants to share perspectives, test ideas, and identify areas of consensus and disagreement.
